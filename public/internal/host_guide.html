Host Guide: Street
Interview Shoots.

You run the interview: pull real reactions, explain the product cleanly, and close hard. The videographer runs the camera. Read this before every shoot.

00 Your job

The reaction is
all that matters.

The whole video lives or dies on the reaction you pull out of the interviewee: the laugh, the "oh honestly...", the surprise. Explaining the product cohesively and closing with a strong CTA is the baseline the client pays for. A genuine reaction is what makes the video outperform, and it's the single thing that decides good vs. forgettable. Everything below exists to pull one out.

Your job

You own three things at once:

  • Pull out real reactions. Create a safe space so interviewees speak and react honestly. This is the job: validate every answer and follow up until they're telling a story (Section 3).
  • Bridge and explain the product cohesively. Cover every key point the brief requires. This is an ad, so during the ad read: no stuttering, no filler words (Sections 4-5).
  • Close with a strong call to action (Section 6).

01 Pre-shoot

  • Make sure both mics are charged.
  • One mic attached to you, one for the interviewee.
Mic stays on them

Keep the interviewee's mic on them, close and steady, the whole time they talk. Don't pull it back toward yourself when you speak: you have your own mic, and swapping it makes the levels uneven. An amazing video is ruined if their audio isn't captured cleanly, and audio you miss can't be fixed in the edit.

02 Approach · before you film

Walk up in a friendly way. Ask for a couple of minutes to answer a few questions on camera. Get the yes before you start filming, unless otherwise specified.

03 Pull the reaction

This is where the video is won. Your goal is a genuine reaction and a story, not just an answer.

  • Break the ice first · first ~10 seconds. Before the real questions, easy small talk: their name, where they're from. It loosens them up and warms the energy.
  • Ask the hook question.
  • Always validate their answer ("love that", "so true"). It keeps them comfortable and open the whole way through.
  • Ask follow-up questions to get them diving into a story, even if it drifts from the brief for a moment. The reaction is worth it.
Never overlap

Always let them cut you off and talk over you: the moment they start, stop talking. And leave about 1 second of silence between responses. In the edit we can easily push two clips together, but we cannot separate two voices talking at the same time.

04 Bridge to the product

Move from their story into the product without breaking the spell. Don't hard-cut into a sales pitch: connect the product to what they just said so it never feels like an ad dropped on top of the conversation. Use their own words as the on-ramp.

05 Product intro & value props

  • Explain the product cohesively: complete sentences, all the key points. You're on film and mistakes get cut, so clean delivery matters.
  • You don't have to read the script word for word. Unless the brief explicitly requires exact wording, say it naturally in your own words: but only in a way that hits every key point. Natural beats robotic, as long as nothing gets dropped.
  • Give them space to respond and react before and after you hit value props. The reaction is still the point, even during the ad read.
  • Cover all the relevant value props the brief requires. It's fine to pull out your phone to check the brief mid-interview: better a slightly awkward moment than to miss key points.
The restart rule

No stuttering or filler. When a stutter happens, DO NOT finish your sentence. Stop and restart from the last comma or the start of the sentence, so the editor never has to cut filler words out of the middle of your line.

06 Close with the CTA

  • Finish with a strong CTA. This is what gets viewers to take action instead of continuing to scroll.
  • Same rule: no stutters. Restate the sentence until you finish it in one go. Refer to the brief mid-interview as needed to hit the points.
Always end with this

"Any final thoughts for the viewers at home?" (or a follow-up variation). Reactions are what determine whether a video is good, and this question pulls the last, often best, one out of them.

Then keep the conversation going. Even after the interview "ends", entertain small talk and keep joking: people drop their guard once they think it's over, and that's when the best reactions slip out. The videographer keeps filming, so stay in it.
