Guide: Street
Interview Shoots.

Everything that has to go right on camera, for whoever runs the phone. Open this on your phone at the shoot. · 2026-07-02

00 You're the producer

Your job

The actor is walking up to total strangers: the hard, exposed part of the shoot. You own three things at once:

  • Keep everyone comfortable. Make the actor feel at ease, especially as they approach strangers, and keep positive energy so the interviewees loosen up too.
  • Capture the whole moment. Get all the meat of the reactions: the laugh, the pause, the surprise. Don't cut early or look away, the gold is in the reaction.
  • Keep the footage beautiful. It should be pleasing to the eye: well-framed, well-lit, steady. The rest of this guide is how you hit that bar.

You're part of the crew, not just the lens: you can approach people yourself to line up interviews. It doesn't all have to fall on the host.

01 Pre-shoot

  • Charge everything. Make sure the phone and both mics are fully charged before the shoot. Bring a portable charger for both as needed.
  • Free up phone storage. Make sure you have enough space on the phone for the whole shoot.
  • Do an audio test before filming. Record a few seconds and play it back to confirm the mics are capturing clean audio.

02 Choosing the spot: off the walkway

Set up to the side of the sidewalk, not in the direct walkway, or people will keep walking between your camera and the subjects. Stand the interviewer and interviewee side by side, both facing the camera, so neither one blocks the other.

03 Backdrop: not just a wall

Point the camera so there's an attractive backdrop behind the subjects: a lively street, storefronts, greenery, a plaza. A plain blank wall makes the video look flat and cheap.

Diagram 2 Pick a backdrop · what the camera sees
Don't back them against a bare wall · Do put life and depth behind them

04 Light: sun behind the camera

Keep the sun at your back so it lights the subjects' faces. Shoot into the sun and you get glare, a blown-out background, and faces in shadow.

Diagram 3 Sun behind the camera · top-down view
Don't point the lens at the sun · Do keep the sun at your back

05 Framing: full body, vertical, steady

  • Shoot vertical (9:16). Hold the phone upright: these run on TikTok, Reels, and Shorts.
  • Fit the entire body of both the interviewer and the interviewee in frame.
  • Keep it steady. Brace your elbows against your body or use a gimbal, no jerky moves. Shaky footage is what makes phone video look cheap.
  • Re-frame as they move. People drift while talking, so keep adjusting to keep both bodies fully in frame.
Diagram 4 Full-body framing
Don't crop heads or feet · Do stand back, fit both head-to-toe

06 Mics

Use two mics: one clipped to the host, and one designated for the interviewees so their audio is always captured. Put the wind muffs on both. Before shooting, make sure both mics are connected and the mic indicator is showing connected.

07 While rolling

  • Watch the PHONE screen, not the live conversation. Your eyes stay on what the camera is capturing: the recording is the only thing that exists afterward.
  • Keep the positive energy going so the interviewee stays comfortable.
  • Keep rolling after the interview "ends." Don't cut the second they wrap. The realest, funniest reactions often come once they think it's over, so keep filming through the goodbye. This one is about catching gold, not the angle.

08 After the shoot

Upload all the footage to the Google Drive folder we provide for the shoot. Don't leave it only on the phone, and don't delete anything until it's confirmed uploaded.
