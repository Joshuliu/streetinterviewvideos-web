Field SOP · Editor Guide

Editor Guide: Street
Interview Videos.

You turn a raw street interview into a short, scroll-stopping ad. This is the format, layer by layer. Aimed at a new editor working in CapCut.

00 Your job

Imagine your viewer is a 10-year-old
with a horrible attention span.

You take raw interview footage and cut it into a tight 30-50 second ad. Every single second has to earn the next one: if a moment isn't pulling the viewer forward, it gets cut. It should feel like a real conversation, not a host reading an ad, and you order everything so interest builds to a peak by the end.

You build the video in layers, in order
1Select: pick the footage with a full arc + the right people
2Base cut: the 30-50s backbonemost important
3SFX + B-roll: emphasize points, keep stimulation high
4Captions + Music: the finishing layer
5Export: 9:16 1080 × 1920 MP4, into the Drive folder, on time

01 Select the footage

Selecting is a weighing game, and you weigh the reaction heaviest. The host should reliably deliver the product intro, value props, and CTA, so treat those as a baseline. What actually makes one clip better than another is the interviewee's reaction. You don't have to score every clip literally, but keep these priorities in mind when choosing footage (it's also the rubric our AI selection uses):

Scoring appearance

"Looks the part" means they fit the brand's audience and vibe: a perfume wants attractive, flirty energy; a sports-betting app wants charismatic, confident personalities. Skip anyone off-brand or unpolished. You're casting the face of the ad, so lead with the people who make the brand look good.

Mashups. When the video you're editing is a mashup, you combine the best parts across several interviews: if interview A has the strongest hook and interview B has the strongest CTA, use A's hook and B's CTA.

02 Base cut · the make-or-break layer

Cut the selected footage into the tight 30-50s backbone. This layer is what separates a good editor from a bad one, so it's where you earn your keep.

  • Prep the clips first. Before you break anything up, run voice enhancement on the audio and auto-adjust on the footage across all the clips, so everything looks and sounds clean.
  • Storytelling is the core skill. When someone shares a story, find the few beats that make it land concisely and cut the rest. If a story doesn't grip or build toward the product, drop it: a tight story beats a complete one.
  • The brief is your checklist. Hit every point it says to mention, and avoid everything it says to avoid.

Mute swear words · paid-ads safety

These run as paid ads. A single audible curse word can get the video flagged or rejected by the ad platform, which harms the whole campaign. As you cut, fully mute the audio over every swear. (You'll censor it in the caption too, in the captions layer.)

Then work the arc top to bottom:

  • Start directly with the hook. No windup.
  • Keep only the most interesting parts of the stories. Cut filler and pauses.
  • Prefer clean audio, but it's not strict. Lean away from choppy or rough segments; if the words are really good, keep the bit anyway.
  • Bridge naturally into the product introduction.
  • Use the host's most fluent sentences, and cover all the important brief points.
  • Prioritize extra reactions from the interviewee before the CTA.
  • Make sure the CTA is strong.
  • Feel free to reorganize parts to land a stronger message. You're not locked to the filmed order.
  • Use zoom on purpose (dynamic + keyframe push-ins). Two reasons: guide the viewer's focus to whoever's speaking (keeps the 10-year-old locked in), and make two spliced clips feel like one natural moment instead of an obvious cut.
  • Use L-cuts to smooth the audio between clips. Let one clip's audio carry a beat under the next clip's video so spliced sections transition seamlessly, not abruptly. what's an L-cut?

03 SFX + B-roll

Add sound effects and b-roll (stickers, images, sub-videos, emojis) to emphasize points and keep the stimulation high.

  • Sound effects on b-roll animations for extra stimulation, and to exaggerate something funny or wild. Royalty-free / commercially-licensed SFX only.
  • B-roll to visualize a point. "France wins the World Cup, or a dream date with Shakira?" lands far better with a France emoji + "OR" + a Shakira image.
  • Reinforce the product visually at three beats: when it's introduced, during the value props, and again at the CTA. Don't reuse the exact same clip, match the visual to each moment.
  • What you show varies by brand. A perfume might show the bottle at the intro and the logo at the CTA; an app might show the logo at the intro, in-app footage during the value props, and an app-store download animation at the CTA.
  • Keep b-roll text and logos in the safe zone. Plain b-roll can fill the frame, but anything the viewer should read (a logo, an app-store badge, a text overlay) follows the same safe-zone rule as captions (section 4).

04 Captions + Music · the finishing layer

The last layer.

  • Captions come up as phrases, not word-by-word. Simple fonts, neutral colors.
  • New with us? Start with CapCut's default auto captions, not a caption template. Templates, especially the word-for-word highlight kind, are hard to time right, and every word you retype inside one is a new chance for an error. Get accurate captions down first: default captions, restyled with a different font or style if you like. Once you're consistently accurate, templates open up as an option.
  • No glowing caption styles. Glow effects look unprofessional in an ad. Stick to clean, flat text.
  • Pick one style and stay consistent: ALL UPPERCASE or proper sentence case (capitalized and punctuated normally). Either works, unless specified otherwise.
  • Split captions between two speakers. CapCut likes to lump both people into one caption, so break them apart.
  • Music: match who's watching (World Cup content uses World Cup-style audio). No lyrics. Make sure it starts and ends naturally with the clip. Cleared for commercial use only: these are paid ads, so a copyright claim can pull the whole video.
Keep all text in the safe zone

On Reels, the like/comment/share buttons, the account name, and the caption cover the edges of the screen. Anything the viewer needs to read (captions, text overlays, logos, CTAs) must sit inside the middle safe zone, or it gets covered. Keep the top ~14% and bottom ~35% clear, plus ~6% on each side. B-roll is more flexible: if it has no text the viewer needs to read, it can fill the frame. When a caption and b-roll share the screen, keep the caption over an empty part of the b-roll or move it clear, staying inside the safe zone.

Captions have no wiggle room

The earlier layers reward creative diversity. Captions don't: a caption mistake isn't a style choice, it's just wrong, and it looks unprofessional to the client and to their viewers. These are mandatory:

  • Every question ends with "?" in both styles.
  • Sentence case still means proper case: capitalize the start of every sentence and every brand name, and use full punctuation. (doordash → DoorDash)
  • Hyphenate compounds. (must have → must-have)
  • Spell homophones right. (theres / their → there's / theirs / they're)
Censor swear words in the caption

You already muted the audio over swears in the base cut. Match it in the caption: don't half-mask them (not "f***ing", not "f- guy"), either drop the word ("this guy") or fully blank it ("this ___ guy"). The caption and the muted audio must line up.

05 Export & deliver

  • Tool: CapCut.
  • Export vertical 9:16, 1080 × 1920, MP4.
  • Upload every finished file to Google Drive.
  • We'll specify the deliverables (how many videos, which angles, singles vs mashups) per order.
Deliver on time

Late deliveries cascade: they push our delivery to the client, which pushes their campaign timing, which really hurts for time-sensitive content (current events, a live tournament). Only commit to a turnaround you can reliably hit, with buffer built in.

Fast + reliable is best. Slower + reliable is good. Fast + unreliable is the worst.
